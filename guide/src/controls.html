DomLoom guide

Controls

Every control here is the browser's own element. DomLoom recognises it inside a stage and draws how it moves; its value, validation, keyboard and accessibility are untouched. Entry: dom-loom/forms (choices, ranges, fields, numbers) and dom-loom/core (buttons and hold), or dom-loom for all.

Buttons and focus

A press dents a <button>, a <summary>, a link or the label of a radio or checkbox around the point of contact; the release sends a ring through it. Lock-on brackets fly to the control you point at or tab to: the last input wins, so Tab takes them from the mouse.

Press near a button's edge, then near its middle: the dent follows the finger. Then use Tab and Shift+Tab.

class="loom-primary" (or type="submit") marks the main action of a panel in the default skin.

Hold to confirm

hold="900ms" makes a button fire only after a hold, with the pointer or with Space or Enter on it. It charges, shakes, and bursts when it fires; a tap is refused with a shake. An activation that nobody pressed (a screen reader's, Enter in a field) arms it instead and says "activate again to confirm": a second activation within 4 seconds confirms, so the button works for assistive technology that can only send a click.

Tap it: it refuses. Hold it for 1.2 seconds. With the keyboard, focus it and hold Space.

Radios, checkboxes and switches

Radios that share a name get a slanted selection that slides to the new choice, front edge first; while it moves it is glass, and the labels it slides over bend at its edges. A checkbox fills from the centre. A checkbox with role="switch" gets a knob that stretches as it travels.

Jump from Story to Brutal and back. Use the arrow keys inside the group.

Ranges and intervals

An <input type="range"> gets a liquid fill that sloshes with the thumb's speed, and a diamond thumb that brightens while dragged. Two ranges inside an element with interval share one track with two thumbs that cannot cross; the liquid runs between them. Give each its own accessible name.

Drag the field of view fast and let go: the liquid sloshes. Push one interval thumb into the other.

Text fields and validation

Text fields, <textarea> and <select> get a scan line on focus and an underline that pulses while you type. When the browser finds a field invalid (on submit, or when you leave a field you changed), it glitches. Validation is the browser's own: required, pattern, type="email", minlength and the rest.

Press Create profile with the form empty. Then type a gamertag with a space in it and leave the field.

Numbers

Stepping an <input type="number"> rolls its digits over: the old value leaves, the new one comes in. The arrow keys and the wheel step it, and so does a button with aria-controls set to its id and data-step="1" (or -1): a number of steps of the field's step.

Press + a few times quickly, then − .

A field with step="any" has no step of its own, so it takes amounts in its own units: data-step on a button is the amount (data-step="0.5" adds 0.5), and data-step-by on the field is what an arrow key or a notch of the wheel adds (1 by default). The value lands on that amount's grid in the direction of the step (105 + 10 gives 110, not 115), stays within min and max, and keeps the amount's decimals: 0.2 + 0.1 is 0.3.

Press +: 12.35 goes to 12.5, the next half tonne. Then focus the field and use the arrow keys: tenths.

Select and suggestions

A browser's own popups cannot be drawn into a canvas, so a stage opens its own. A <select> opened with the pointer shows its list inside the stage; from the keyboard or a screen reader it keeps the browser's popup, which is fully accessible. An <input list> with its <datalist> becomes a combobox (the ARIA pattern: arrow keys, Enter, Escape) whose list unfolds under the field.

Click Region. Type "d" in Map and use the arrow keys.

Stars

rating on a <fieldset> of radios draws them as stars: pointing previews, choosing lights them up one after another. Give each radio an accessible name.

Move along the stars, then choose five.

File drop

dropzone on an element holding an <input type="file">: files dragged over it pull the panel into a vortex at the pointer; dropped, they go to the input (its change event fires) and the vortex collapses. Clicking it still opens the file picker.

Drag a file from your desktop over the panel, move around, and drop it.
