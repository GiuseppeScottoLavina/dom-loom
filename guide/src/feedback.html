DomLoom guide

Feedback and data

Bars, tabs, disclosures, tables and notifications, written with the HTML and ARIA you already know. DomLoom reacts when they change, whoever changes them: your script, the user, a framework.

Meters and progress

<meter> and <progress> become game bars. A drop cuts the bar at once, sends a shock through the whole stage and leaves a white trail that drains after a beat; a gain shows where the bar is going and fills toward it. Meters are cut into chips (segments="5" sets how many, segments="0" none) and take the colour of their region from low, high and optimum, as HTML defines them. A <progress> without a value sweeps; reaching the end flashes.

Take a hit twice: the bar turns amber, then red. Heal. Press Sync save.

Add ring to a <progress> or a <meter> for a dial with the same trail, gain preview, segments and sweep.

Tabs

The ARIA tabs pattern works as written: click, the arrow keys, Home and End select a tab, and the panels are shown and hidden with hidden. The indicator stretches to the new tab, and the panel changes with a cut that runs the way you moved. Use <button type="button" role="tab">, give each tab an id and aria-controls, and each panel role="tabpanel" and aria-labelledby.

Click Lore, then Stats: the cut runs the way you move. Focus a tab and use the arrow keys.

Details

Opening a <details> slides what is below down and uncovers the body behind a scan line; closing shuts it upward and its last rows crumble. Clicks during the slide reach what is shown. Details that share a name (an exclusive accordion) switch with a cut.

Open Repair the foghorn: the first one closes, and the line below moves with them.

Sortable tables

<table sortable> makes every header of the first <thead> row a sort button (nosort on a <th> opts out). Rows sort by data-value or by their text, numbers as numbers, and fly to their new places on arcs. A click on a flying row reaches it. The table fires loom-sort first (cancelable, to sort on a server or in a framework's state) and loom-sorted once the rows are in order.

Sort by Score, then by Level, and click a row while it flies.

Pages and steps

Mark where the user is with aria-current, as you would anyway: aria-current="page" on the current page of a <nav> list or on the last place of a nav[aria-label=breadcrumb], and aria-current="step" on the current step of an <ol>. When it moves, the marker stretches over; a stepper's beam runs up to the current step. Your script moves the attribute.

Go to page 3, then press Next step a few times.

Your app's own navigation (a top bar) is not a pagination: put current="line" on the nav (or its list). The links keep your look, and the current one gets a line on its bottom edge that slides to the next page. In a bar, wrap the nav in <loom-stage bare fill>: no panel, the bar's colour behind it, and the bar's height. The reticle follows the pointer and the keyboard there, inside the bar; reticle="focus" on the nav keeps it to keyboard focus.

Go from Lobby to Ranks: the line slides along the bottom of the bar.

Toasts, alerts, badges, busy

  • toasts on a list or a box: each child is a toast. It assembles, counts down (data-timeout, 5 s by default) with a bar of light, holds while pointed at or focused, and crumbles when its time is up. data-tone="good", "warn" or "bad" colours it. The region is made a polite live region. toasts="viewport" is for a page's notifications: put the list in a bare stage of its own, and it sits at the bottom centre of the viewport, above the page, across the width of a phone.
  • role="alert": when an alert appears or its text changes, it lands with a shock and its text tears for a moment.
  • badge: when its number changes, it pings; going up and going down sound and light differently.
  • aria-busy="true": the placeholder content is drawn as blocks with a scan over them; when it is no longer busy, the real content assembles block by block.

Press Player joins: the badge pings and a toast counts down; point at it to hold it. Press Boss incoming. Roll the loot.

Removable items

removable on a tag, a chip or a list item gives it a remove button; Delete or Backspace on the item work too. Removing crumbles it and the rest flies into the gap. The item fires loom-remove first (cancelable: a framework cancels it and removes the item from its own state). A removable item that is added assembles block by block. exit="shatter" breaks it into shards instead (entry dom-loom/shatter).

removable draws the item as a chip. removable="row" keeps the look of an item that is not a chip (a table row, a grid row) and only styles its remove button. An item that has its own button.loom-remove keeps that button's label and content.

Remove Ranked, then No bots (it shatters). Add a filter of your own.
