DomLoom guide

Skins and theming

A skin decides how a stage looks, moves and sounds. The behaviours (pressing, holding, selecting, sorting) are the same for every skin: they compute what each control is doing and hand that state to the skin's draw hooks, which turn it into compositor primitives. A skin also has a stylesheet for the controls at rest. holo is the default.

Recolour holo

Holo reads its colours and fonts from custom properties on the stage: --loom-holo (light), --loom-signal (selection and danger), --loom-warn, --loom-ink, --loom-dim, --loom-panel, --loom-line, --loom-void (the page around the panel), --loom-cut (the size of the cut corners), --loom-font, --loom-display and --loom-mono. Set them in your stylesheet; the GPU picks them up.

Switch the mode, drag the volume and hold Launch: the same motion, in other colours.

Keep --loom-void the colour of the page behind the stage, so its cut corners blend in.

A skin of your own

defineSkin registers a skin by name; a stage uses it with skin="name", and picks it up even if it was registered later. tokens maps colour names to a custom property and a fallback; the draw hooks receive them, as RGB, in ctx.colors. roles tells the compositor which colours to use for its own drawing (the cut, its edge, the panel, empty space). A hook the skin leaves out draws nothing, and the skin's stylesheet styles the controls at rest: scope it to loom-stage[skin="name"].

Press and hold a button, then move between them with the mouse and with Tab.

The hooks and the state each one receives are listed in the README; ctx also holds now, reduced (reduced motion: skip shakes), whole (the stage's rect), durations and stage.

Extend a skin

extendSkin(name, hooks) replaces or adds draw hooks of a skin, for every stage that uses it. To keep what a hook did, call the original from yours. This one lights the bottom edge of a button as it sinks, only on buttons marked data-lit-edge.

Press and hold Marked, then Plain.

Behaviours and primitives can be written too: defineBehavior for what a control does, and definePrimitive for a new way to draw, with its WGSL and the JavaScript twin that keeps input mapped. The DomLoom skill and the types in dom-loom/core describe the contracts.
