DomLoom guide

Effects

One attribute on an ordinary element. Each of these works on the pixels of the live page, and the content stays clickable where it is drawn: a click on a tilted card, a magnified button or stirred text lands on what you see.

Tilting cards

tilt on a card turns it toward the pointer in perspective, with a shadow and a glare; in the default skin its foil lights up the edges of the content as it turns. Its buttons still work where they are drawn.

Move over the card slowly, then press Equip while it is turned.

Carousels

carousel on a scroll container makes a scroll-snapping strip that the mouse can drag. Buttons with aria-controls set to its id and data-step="1" (or -1) move it a slide. While it moves it curves like film, the more the faster. Touch and the trackpad scroll it as usual.

Drag the strip fast and let go. Then use the arrows.

Splitters

split on an element holding a pane, an <hr> and a pane turns the <hr> into a window splitter. Drag it, or focus it and use the arrow keys, Home and End; set its range and position with aria-valuemin, aria-valuemax and aria-valuenow, in percent. Pulled past a limit, the panes stretch like rubber and spring back.

Pull the splitter all the way to one side and let go. Focus it with Tab and press Home.

Magnifying glass

loupe on a table, a map or a block of small text gives it a glass that follows the mouse: loupe="3" sets the magnification (2 by default) and loupe-size="160" its diameter in CSS pixels. The glass really magnifies the picture, and a click inside it lands on the magnified element. On touch, press and hold shows the glass above the finger; a plain tap is left alone.

Move the glass over the small text, then over the button, and click it through the glass.

A point light

lit on a bordered panel makes the pointer a point light: text and borders emboss under it. lit="0.6" sets the intensity (1 by default). It moves no pixel, so the inputs inside keep working as usual.

Sweep the pointer across the panel slowly.

Stirring like water

fluid on a readout panel lets the pointer stir its content like water, and it settles by itself. fluid="0.5" sets the strength. Put it on panels that show things, not on panels full of sliders or fields, which a drag would fight. The default skin sets touch-action: pan-y pinch-zoom on it, so a finger stirs sideways and still scrolls and zooms the page.

Drag across the panel and watch it settle. Click Surface while the water still moves.
