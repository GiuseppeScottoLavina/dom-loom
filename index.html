Web components on HTML-in-Canvas

Forms with game feel.

DomLoom draws ordinary HTML controls through one WebGPU compositor. Presses dent, selections slide, focus locks on, and the elements underneath stay real HTML: focusable, readable by screen readers, and submitted with the form.

  • Hold Deploy until it fires. A tap is not enough.
  • Press Tab to move through the form. The brackets follow focus.
  • Leave Callsign empty and deploy. The field glitches.
  • Drag Drop altitude fast and let go.

Still plain HTML

Damage you can read.

Bars are <meter> and <progress>, objectives are <details>, and the tabs are the ARIA tabs pattern. Change a value from your code and the compositor shows how it changed.

  • Take a hit. The hull drops at once, a shock bends the whole panel, and the white trail drains after a beat.
  • Open the uplink. The bar sweeps until there is a value, then fills to a flash.
  • Switch tabs with the arrow keys. The cut runs the way you move and the old panel crumbles ahead of it.
  • Open an objective. What is below slides down, and a scan line uncovers the brief.
  • Scope the map on Intel. The glass reads what is too small to see. Click a mark for its brief.

Sort it. Watch it move.

A <table sortable> sorts by any column, and its rows fly to their new places along arcs. They stay clickable in flight. Pages, breadcrumbs and steppers are plain lists with aria-current.

  • Sort by Health twice. The bars travel with their rows.
  • Go to page 2. The marker stretches over and the rows are cut in.
  • Tab to a column header and press Enter. Sorting is a real button.

Inputs

Every value has weight.

A number field, a pair of ranges, a <datalist>, a file input, a radio group. Their values roll over, files pull the panel into a vortex, and chips crumble when you remove them.

  • Press + on Ammo crates. The digits roll over.
  • Drag a file from your desktop onto the drop zone. The panel twists toward it.
  • Remove a tag, then add one. It crumbles; the new one assembles.
  • Type in Extraction point. The list unfolds inside the stage.

Feedback

Things happen. You notice.

Toasts, alerts, badges, busy regions and ring gauges, all written with HTML and ARIA you already know: role="alert", aria-busy, <progress ring>. DomLoom reacts when they change, whoever changes them.

  • Ping the squad. A toast counts down; point at it to hold it. The reply bumps the badge.
  • Trigger the alarm. It lands with a shock and the signal ring takes the hit.
  • Decrypt the intercept. The placeholder scans until the text assembles.
